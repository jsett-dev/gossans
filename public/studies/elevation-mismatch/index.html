Filed elevations that do not sit on the ground

How many wells hang from a filed elevation that disagrees with the measured ground beneath them, and what explains the disagreement?

36,467 wells · rebuilt 2026-09-18 · every figure from public filings.
FoundIn one paragraph

36467 wells in the region carry a filed elevation and sit on the measured grid. The median well is -9.5 m off the ground; 7566 wells, 20.7% of them, are more than 100 m off, and 5595 of those are filed below the ground rather than above it. The commonest thing the numbers support: shared with at least three neighbours: systematic over the area (6984 wells).

PopulationWho is counted

Located wells in the region's fourteen counties with at least one formation top carrying a usable filed elevation, where the USGS grid has a value under the well.

beyond 100 m 7566 · quartiles m [-60.3, -9.5, 28.8]

MethodHow each number was made
  • The filed elevation is the median of the elevations filed with the well's tops, after discarding values outside the bounds a Wyoming or Montana elevation can take.
  • The ground is the USGS 3DEP elevation on the site's own grid, 800 m across, at the cell containing the filed surface location; the relief is the range across that cell and its eight neighbours.
  • A well is mismatched when the filed elevation differs from that ground by more than 100 m, which is past what a kelly bushing can account for.
  • Explanations are tried in order: feet where metres were meant, metres where feet were meant, the well's own tops disagreeing by more than 300 ft, a mismatch no larger than the relief around the cell, a mismatch shared within 50 m by at least three other mismatched wells within about two kilometres, else a slip of the well's own.

CaveatsWhat this does not show
  • An 800 m grid can differ from the true ground at a point by tens of metres in rough country. That is why the threshold is a hundred metres and not ten, and why the relief test comes before the others that could claim a well.
  • A kelly bushing or rig floor sits a few metres above the ground; a filed elevation a few metres high is right, not wrong. Most mismatches here are low, not high.
  • A pattern shared across a neighbourhood does not say which side is wrong. The grid over that area and the filings over that area are both candidates, and the one-metre elevation the scene already fetches can settle it well by well.
  • Nothing here is corrected in the store or on the site. The scene hangs every well from its filed elevation where one exists.
